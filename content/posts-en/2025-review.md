Looking back at 2025, it was a year of **steady refinement paired with a deliberate effort to expand my cognitive boundaries**.
My work focus shifted from data infrastructure and foundational systems in the first half of the year to refining processes, standards, and the ability to drive complex projects end-to-end in the second half. At the same time, I intentionally avoided confining my technical growth to a single vertical, building intersections across system performance, data modeling, AI, and interdisciplinary knowledge.

This year reinforced a simple conviction: **long-term value comes not from accumulating a list of skills, but from compounding abstraction and judgment.**

---

## 🏢 Work Review: From "Shipping Tasks" to "Building Methodology"

### 🟢 First Half: Data Infrastructure and Standardization

In the first half of the year, my main focus was on foundational work: **data ingestion, data modeling, and centralized storage design**.

I participated in and drove the ingestion and rollout of multi-source heterogeneous data, covering structured data, news and feed data, and raw file-based data. Throughout this process, I looked beyond whether the data was merely "usable" and kept thinking about its cost in future scaling, maintenance, and collaboration.

During table design and implementation, I tried to leave clear structures and boundaries beyond just "meeting today's requirements," so the models could support future evolution instead of becoming throwaway artifacts.

At the same time, I pushed for and implemented several standardization practices:

- Clarifying directory structures and delivery conventions in data development to reduce hidden friction in collaboration
- Unifying foundational classifications and standards to lay a stable base for downstream analysis and reuse

This kind of work rarely draws attention, but it makes a real difference to a team's long-term efficiency and system health.

---

### 🔵 Second Half: Process Governance and Complex Projects

In the second half of the year, my attention gradually shifted from single-point delivery to **whether the process structure itself made sense**.

### 1. Team Processes and Engineering Governance

I took the initiative to push a series of process-level improvements:

- Standardizing release notes and changelogs to reduce uncertainty as versions evolve
- Advocating for a unified intake channel for requirements and feedback, cutting down on information loss from relying solely on ad-hoc chat

None of these changes aimed for instant results. The goal was **to keep team efficiency from depending heavily on individual experience and memory**.

### 2. High-Complexity, Large-Scale Data Project

In a representative large-scale data project, I was responsible for the overall solution design and key technical decisions.

Facing massive data volume, I focused on the essence of the requirement rather than stockpiling data out of technical inertia. After multiple rounds of alignment with stakeholders, we settled on a data modeling strategy centered on the "latest state," and held firm to that principle throughout implementation.

During reviews and execution, I consistently prioritized explainability, maintainability, and long-term cost over adding complexity for a false sense of short-term safety.

That phase taught me a clear lesson: **good architectural decisions often show up in what you choose not to do.**

---

## 🚀 Technical Growth: Deepening Foundations, Expanding Breadth

### 🧠 Depth: Data Structures and System Design

This year, I intentionally dug deeper into "foundational yet high-frequency" problems:

- Systematically cataloging and comparing multiple hierarchical and tree-based data modeling approaches, clarifying where each one fits best
- Building a fuller understanding of key phenomena and metrics in high-concurrency systems, rather than stopping at conceptual knowledge
- Putting more attention on infrastructure stability and overall system behavior instead of local performance tweaks

One noticeable shift: **I am far less fixated on individual techniques and much more focused on how a system behaves over long-term operation.**

---

### 🔭 Breadth: Exploring AI and Engineering Practices

While keeping my main work on track, I also carved out room for exploration:

- Following engineering paths that combine graph structures with LLMs, and trying to understand their practical value boundaries
- Picking up new engineering frameworks and development paradigms to broaden my perspective on tooling and architecture choices

These explorations do not always pay off immediately, but they give me a better "noise floor calibration" for future technical judgment.

---

## 📖 Interdisciplinary Reading and Personal Reflections

### 🧬 Interdisciplinary Reading

My reading this year branched noticeably outside of technology:

- Learning from neuroscience about the relationship between "local rules and global emergence" in complex systems
- Re-examining the interplay between technological progress and institutional evolution through the lenses of law and history

This reading does not directly boost short-term output, yet it steadily shapes how I make sense of complex problems.

---

### 📚 Deep Reading and Mental Models

My reading clustered around three main themes:

### 1. Work and Organizational Philosophy

Repeatedly thinking through the difference between busyness and effectiveness, scale and correctness, and revisiting commitments, pacing, and long-term autonomy.

### 2. Mental Models and Uncertainty

Focusing on how to move decisions forward under incomplete information and unclear paths, rather than waiting for ideal conditions.

### 3. Technology, Humanities, and Imagination

Using biographies and fiction to remind myself: **technology did not originally exist for metrics.**

---

## 💭 Fragments of Thought and Life

This year also left a few scattered but important personal takeaways:

- Staying alert to meaningless busywork dressed up as "value"
- Renewed respect for risk, boundaries, and safety
- A more rational perspective on investing and future uncertainty

They may not form neat conclusions, but together they have become the underlying coordinates for how I judge my life.

---

## 📝 Closing

Looking back at 2025, two words sum it up best for me: **structure** and **boundaries**.

At work, I gradually shifted from someone who completes tasks to someone who thinks in systems and processes;
in technology, I stopped chasing short-term trends and kept honing the problems that show up again and again over time.

**Looking Ahead to 2026**:
Keep investing patiently in first principles, and look for real leverage inside seemingly dry systems and data;
stay calm and unhurried, breaking complex problems down into structures that can be built out over the long haul.

Here is to a new year with steadier systems, clearer judgment, and a calmer pace.
