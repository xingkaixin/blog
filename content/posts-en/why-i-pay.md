## Origin

I saw people on Weibo criticizing users who install ad blockers for Youku, calling it completely wrong and pointing out that these extensions could also pose potential privacy risks.

## Paying for Youku

A few years ago, whenever I wanted to watch a video, I would hunt for a download link on various sites, wait for it to finish downloading, and then watch it. A major downside of that approach is that your backlog of unwatched downloads keeps growing until you end up deleting files just to free up hard drive space. People who download Blu-ray HD videos via private trackers (PT) today probably still run into the same thing. Online video sites solved that problem nicely: you search for what you want and start watching right away without a long wait, and with broadband speeds improving steadily over the past few years, video quality has become much less of an issue. So sites like Youku really did solve a huge problem for users. Then came the question of Youku's own survival. Building the platform takes massive resources, and at Youku's scale, buying bandwidth from ISPs is an enormous expense. Over the past few years, pre-roll ads on Youku grew from 15 seconds to a full minute. On top of that, many domestic ads are repetitive and uninspired, so people grew to hate watching them. That pushback led to ad-blocking extensions like BlockAD. Later, Youku introduced paid memberships to remove ads—a reasonable approach in theory. The problem is that many users reject a heavy-handed model that degrades the experience first and charges to fix it.

## How Evernote Does It

With Evernote, free users get a 60 MB monthly upload limit, while paid tiers add searchable PDFs and faster image text recognition. On the desktop app, there is a small ad in the bottom-left corner, and the mobile app lacks passcode lock and offline notebooks. For casual, non-power users, these missing features barely make a difference. Even as a paying Evernote user, I rarely hit the 60 MB monthly upload limit. The tiny ad only really stands out on a low-resolution laptop screen. On mobile, I do use offline notebooks—offline downloads are genuinely useful—though I don't need them all the time, especially with 3G coverage already fairly widespread across China. Even though the day-to-day experience for an average user is practically the same whether they pay or not, the company became profitable back in 2011 and its revenue is growing rapidly.
I read an analysis noting that a product like Evernote, which stores your memories, builds strong user stickiness: the longer you use it, the more you rely on it, and the more willing you become to pay.

## Who Foots the Bill

So for sites like Youku, does lacking that kind of stickiness mean it is hard to get users to pay willingly? Under their current model, yes. Still, is there a better way for a site to make money without hurting the user experience? A product has two potential payers: advertisers, who buy ad placements on the platform to boost brand awareness and sales, and users, who pay extra in exchange for a better service. Lengthening mandatory ad breaks to protect advertisers' interests is understandable, but it inevitably degrades the experience for regular users—especially when ads on domestic video sites are so unwatchable, making people resent them even more. What about value-added paid tiers? There is a quirk specific to China: there are several competing video platforms, and each has its own paid membership. Most users won't subscribe to multiple platforms of the same type, yet because of licensing fragmentation, you inevitably have to go to another site to watch what you want—and suffer through terrible ads all over again. With that in mind, you might decide not to pay for any of them. That is certainly why I haven't subscribed to any video platform's paid tier so far. Sometimes, when an industry hasn't consolidated around a clear leader, users end up getting a mix of good and bad experiences.

## Closing

When you use a product often, enjoy using it, and paying for a premium tier genuinely gives you a better experience, it is well worth spending the money.
