On the last day of the holiday, I was organizing my Gmail inbox. After clearing out a bunch of useless labels, I stumbled across some old emails chatting casually with old friends. Reading through them one by one left me with a rush of emotions.

Ten years ago, I seemed to love group activities and loved expressing myself in front of friends. There was no boundary between work and life; I often stayed up late writing things and looked forward to the next day's work with boundless excitement. I was constantly thinking, immediately sharing half-formed ideas with everyone, and finding joy in it.

Ten years ago, friends kept in touch by email and phone. We arranged hangouts over email and handled work discussions over email. We witnessed Google's exit from mainland China, a major historical event.

Ten years later, with the onset of the pandemic, staying in touch with many friends happens mostly online, and in-person gatherings are few and far between. Many of my thoughts are no longer shared among friends either, remaining confined to work.
