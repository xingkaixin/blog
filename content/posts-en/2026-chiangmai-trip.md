> In April, Chiang Mai has gentle sunlight and a pleasant breeze.

## Arriving in Chiang Mai: Slow Days in the Old City

![Ancient pagoda at Wat Chedi Luang](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/ancient-pagoda.png)

My first stop was the heart of the Old City—**Wat Chedi Luang**. This weathered Lanna-style pagoda stands quietly, its red brick against the blue sky forming the quintessential northern Thai palette. Standing beneath it, time seemed to slow down.

![Temple eaves and greenery](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/temple-greenery.png)

There is more than one temple in the Old City. Around a corner, a large tree framed the temple eaves, and a cat napped under the colonnade. Here, even daydreaming feels like a proper way to spend the afternoon.

## Tha Phae Gate: A Date with the Pigeons

![Feeding pigeons at Tha Phae Gate](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/feeding-pigeons.png)

In the early morning at **Tha Phae Gate Square**, the pigeons were already waiting. Crouch down and hold out a hand, and they gather around without hesitation. That kind of simple joy is hard to find in a big city.

## Nimman Road: Coffee and Music

![A cozy cafe on Nimman Road](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/cozy-cafe.png)

![Live music on Nimman Road](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/nimman-live-music.png)

**Nimman Road** shows another side of Chiang Mai. In the afternoon, I ducked into a corner cafe where a pink teddy bear waved from behind the display window. By dusk, live music drifted out of an open-air bar down the street; people sat in small groups, and life felt like an improvised performance.

## Summer Hanging from the Trees

![A mango tree loaded with fruit](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/mango-tree.png)

Summer in Chiang Mai tastes like mangoes. Roadside mango trees hung heavy with fruit, tempting anyone passing by to pick one. This city always finds a way to turn ordinary moments into small delights.

## Streets in Bloom

![A street lined with flame trees](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/flame-tree-street.png)

![Street life in Chiang Mai](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/street-life.png)

In April, Chiang Mai's **flame trees** are in full bloom. Walking down streets covered in red blossoms, your steps naturally grow lighter. A Shiba Inu wearing headphones sunbathed on a street corner—time here is lazy and sweet.

## Chiang Mai University: Ang Kaew Lake and Green Lawns

![Ang Kaew Lake at Chiang Mai University](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/cmu-ang-kaew-lake.png)

**Ang Kaew Lake** at **Chiang Mai University** is the quietest corner of the campus. The water sits like a mirror, reflecting the distant silhouette of Doi Suthep. Sitting by the lake, it is easy to let the hours slip away on something lovely.

![Green lawn on the Chiang Mai University campus](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/cmu-campus-lawn.png)

The wide lawns and towering old trees on campus are a favorite resting spot for locals. Go for an evening stroll and let the breeze carry every worry away.

## Wat Suan Dok: White Pagodas and Running Feet

![White pagodas at Wat Suan Dok](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/white-pagoda.png)

The cluster of white pagodas at **Wat Suan Dok** gleamed in the sunlight. A child ran across the grass with a small dog trailing behind—one of the most moving sights on any trip, a reminder to go see the wider world.

## Sunset Collector

![Sunset by the pool](/posts/images/2026-chiangmai-trip/sunset-pool.png)

Back at my place in the evening, I waited by the pool for the sunset. The sky turned tangerine and pink as Doi Suthep slowly faded into dusk.

---

Chiang Mai has no jaw-dropping landmarks, yet it holds a quiet magic that makes you want to come back again and again. In this small city, I learned to slow down and let the days bloom.

**Go where the wind blows, and be a free breeze.**
